# Rosie Speech and HeyGen Script Review

Status: Draft for review. Do not generate voice or video from this document until each narration block is approved.

## How to review

Edit the text inside each **Narration** section. That text is intended to be read exactly when the voice is created. The remaining sections provide production direction for captions, chat bubbles, motion graphics, and HeyGen assembly.

Approval options:

- Draft
- Approved with edits

## Shared production direction

- Product: Navigate The Pathway
- Audience: Premedical juniors and seniors
- Language: English
- Orientation: Landscape, 16:9
- Tone: Warm, calm, encouraging, practical, and never childish
- Speaker: Rosie, the Roseman University College of Medicine roadrunner mascot
- Visual palette: Roseman maroon, warm cream, muted gold, desert teal, and soft terracotta
- Accessibility: Open captions, transcript, pause, replay, and skip controls
- Claims: Do not invent credentials, admissions outcomes, or institutional promises
- Privacy: Reinforce that prototype work stays on the current device
- Motion: Keep motion gentle and respect reduced-motion preferences

## Video 1: Welcome to Navigate The Pathway

Status: Draft

- Purpose: Introduce the experience and lower first-use uncertainty
- Target duration: 35 to 40 seconds
- Format: Rosie on screen with light motion graphics and short chat-bubble beats
- Structure: Context, core concept, takeaway
- Current word count: 90 words
- Estimated spoken duration: About 36 seconds at 145 words per minute

### Narration

Hi, I'm Rosie, your guide to Navigate The Pathway. You already have experiences that matter. This is a private practice space where you can track courses and experiences, reflect on compassion and your values, learn with your cohort, and shape the story you may use in your medical school application. Each station explains why the work matters, guides one manageable action, and saves something useful on this device. There are no points, rankings, or perfect routes. You choose where to begin, and you can change direction whenever you need to.

### Chat-bubble and caption beats

1. Make your premedical journey visible.
2. Explore six stations at your pace.
3. See why the work matters.
4. Complete one useful action.
5. Save it for advising and applications.

### Critical on-screen text

- Navigate The Pathway
- Your experiences already matter.
- Private practice space
- Six open stations
- Saved on this device
- Begin setup

### Visual direction

- Open on the warm desert pathway with Rosie welcoming the student.
- Show the six stations as simple labeled points on the map.
- Use one short visual example of an experience becoming a saved reflection.
- End with Rosie pointing toward **Begin setup**.
- Keep ASPIRE and OACA marks static and unaltered.

## Video 2: From Hours to Useful Evidence

Status: Draft

- Purpose: Explain why students should capture a specific moment, not only hours
- Target duration: 20 to 25 seconds
- Format: Motion graphics with Rosie's narration voice
- Structure: Context, core concept, takeaway
- Current word count: 49 words
- Estimated spoken duration: About 20 seconds at 145 words per minute

### Narration

Hours help you remember how much time you spent. Stories help you remember why the experience mattered. Start with one specific moment. Describe what happened, what you noticed, and what changed in your thinking. That small reflection becomes evidence you can use in advising conversations, applications, and future decisions.

### Chat-bubble and caption beats

1. Hours track time.
2. Specific moments preserve meaning.
3. Notice what changed.
4. Save evidence for later.

### Critical on-screen text

- Specific moment
- What changed?
- Useful evidence
- Save reflection

- Begin with a simple hour counter.
- Zoom from the total hours into one illustrated moment.
- Connect the moment to meaning, then to one saved evidence card.
- Use motion graphics only. Rosie does not need to appear on screen.
- Avoid application forms, scores, rankings, and admissions imagery.

## Video 3: Many Ways to Participate

Status: Draft

- Purpose: Normalize low-pressure cohort participation for introverted and extroverted students
- Target duration: 20 to 25 seconds
- Format: Motion graphics with Rosie's narration voice
- Structure: Context, core concept, takeaway
- Current word count: 53 words
- Estimated spoken duration: About 22 seconds at 145 words per minute

### Narration

Your cohort is a group of classmates moving through the same process. Participation does not have to mean speaking first or speaking often. You can observe, encourage, respond, share a resource, or invite one follow-up conversation. Start with the level of interaction that feels useful today. Belonging grows through small, genuine contributions.

### Chat-bubble and caption beats

1. Your cohort shares the process.
2. Observe or encourage.
3. Respond or share.
4. Connect when useful.

### Critical on-screen text

- Observe
- Encourage
- Respond
- Share
- Connect
- Start where you are

### Visual direction

- Show the shared practice message board as a simple, fictional interface.
- Animate several valid paths through participation.
- Do not present one participation mode as better than another.
- Show an observing student as an active participant, not an incomplete participant.
- End with a small supportive interaction between classmates.

## HeyGen handoff after approval

When narration and voice audio are approved:

1. Use the final narration audio as the exact timing source.
2. Preserve the approved wording in captions and transcripts.
3. Use supplied Rosie and Roseman assets only. Do not redraw or alter institutional marks.
4. Use the selected presenter only for the welcome video.
5. Use motion graphics for the reflection and cohort videos.
6. Keep critical on-screen text exactly as approved.
7. Export compressed MP4 and WebM files plus poster images and VTT captions.
8. Do not add third-party tracking or embedded players.
