# Navigate the Pathway

## First-session presentation and playtest plan

**Purpose:** Test whether the product teaches its own value with minimal presenter instruction.
**Target:** Premed juniors and seniors.
**Session length:** 25-35 minutes plus debrief.
**Device priority:** Students use their own phones when possible.

---

## 1. What the presenter should say

Keep the introduction intentionally short:

> This is an early version of a tool designed to help you make sense of your path to medicine. Please open it and follow what it asks. I will not explain where to click. Nothing you enter affects admission, and you may use fictional details during this test. I am watching whether the product explains itself, not whether you know the application process.

Do not preview the feature list. Do not explain the branches. Do not tell students that the “correct” outcome is reflection. The product must earn that understanding.

---

## 2. What students should encounter immediately

### Startup orientation: How this works

Before Screen 1, first-time authenticated students see the four-card orientation from Product Specification section 8.0:

1. What this is.
2. What this isn't.
3. How it's private.
4. The five places you'll live.

Students may skip after Card 1. Skipping must move directly into Screen 1 without blocking setup or creating a warning state. During this playtest, observe whether the orientation reduces “What am I supposed to do here?” confusion without spoiling the interactive discovery that follows.

### Screen 1: Recognition

**Message:** “You have already started your path to medicine.”

Students choose a starting statement:

- I have done a lot but have not kept good records.
- I know my courses but not what experience to pursue next.
- I have meaningful experiences but struggle to write about them.
- I want a stronger support system.
- I am still figuring out where I am.

This immediately communicates that different paths are expected.

### Screen 2: Safety and trust

Use three short cards:

- **Private by default:** You choose what to share.
- **Preparation, not prediction:** This does not decide or estimate admission.
- **Protect people:** Do not enter patient names or identifying details.

### Screen 3: Quick landscape

Ask for broad states, not exhaustive detail:

- courses mapped / partly mapped / not mapped;
- experiences explored;
- records current / scattered / mostly in memory;
- target cycle / unsure;
- bandwidth this month; and
- preferred participation: observe, react, respond, connect, or lead.

### Screen 4: Strengths-first map

Show:

1. **What you already carry** - experiences, commitments, skills, support, or questions.
2. **What this can become** - accurate record, reflection, competency evidence, story, or advisor conversation.
3. **One next move** - not a deficit list.

### Screen 5: Three doors

Offer one recommendation and two alternatives:

- Recover the Evidence.
- Chart the Route.
- Find the Story.
- Explore the Next Door.
- Build the Constellation.
- Quiet Start.
- Make It Sustainable.

The recommendation includes “Why we suggested this.”

### Screen 6: Five-minute artifact quest

Recommended default for the live presentation: **Recover the Evidence**.

Ask the student to recall one experience and enter:

- What was it?
- When did it happen?
- What did you actually do?
- Who was affected by or involved in the work?
- What stayed with you?

Then reveal:

> You just created the beginning of three future tools: an accurate activity record, evidence of what you learned, and a story you can revisit when you write your application.

This is the milestone's most important teaching moment.

### Screen 7: Depth contrast

Show two fictional versions of the same experience:

**Hours-only:** “Volunteered 42 hours at a food pantry.”

**Evidence-rich:** “Helped reorganize intake after noticing that the check-in process was difficult for families arriving from work. Learned to ask before assuming what would make access easier.”

Ask:

> Which version helps future-you remember what mattered, and why?

The point is not that longer prose is better. The point is that role, context, impact, and learning make experience usable.

### Screen 8: Cohort arrival

Offer four equivalent ways to enter:

- Observe today's cohort prompt.
- React to an answer that resonates.
- Use a sentence starter to reply.
- Post one thing you can offer or one thing you need.

Students who choose to observe still complete the arrival step. The interface should distinguish “not participating” from “choosing a lower-disclosure mode.”

### Screen 9: Commitment

Ask for one next move:

- log another experience;
- map one course;
- contact one support person;
- try one study strategy;
- join one cohort prompt; or
- review one old reflection.

Let the student choose reminder timing or no reminder.

---

## 3. What the platform must teach without the presenter

By the end of the flow, students should understand:

- their path may begin in a different place from a classmate's;
- experiences have value beyond hour totals;
- accurate details are easiest to capture close to the event;
- reflection turns activity into evidence of learning and growth;
- application material can be built incrementally;
- quiet, asynchronous participation counts as a legitimate entry into community;
- support and help-seeking are part of preparation for medicine; and
- the tool does not predict admission.

---

## 4. Facilitator observation sheet

Observe behavior; do not rescue unless a student is blocked for more than 60 seconds.

| Moment | Observe | Success signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orientation | Does the student understand what the product is and is not? | Continues or intentionally skips without needing presenter explanation. |
| Entry | Does the student understand the premise? | Begins without asking what the tool is for. |
| Inventory | Are questions answerable without application expertise? | Completes or skips confidently. |
| Starting map | Does it feel personalized and strengths-first? | Student can explain why the recommendation appeared. |
| Artifact quest | Can the student complete it on a phone? | Saves a meaningful artifact in under five minutes. |
| Reveal | Does the experience-application connection land? | Student names at least two future uses for the artifact. |
| Cohort arrival | Do quiet students see a safe option? | Students use more than one participation rung. |
| End | Is the next step clear and realistic? | Student chooses and can restate one next move. |

Record:

- first place the student hesitates;
- first term the student does not understand;
- first moment that feels valuable;
- anything that feels judgmental or admissions-like;
- anything students try to tap that is not interactive;
- whether the keyboard covers content;
- whether students worry about privacy; and
- whether students confuse the experience with an official application.

---

## 5. Student debrief prompts

Ask before explaining the product:

1. What did you think the tool was trying to help you do?
2. What did the tool teach you about tracking experiences?
3. What felt useful, and what felt like extra work?
4. Did the recommended path fit you? Why or why not?
5. Did anything feel like it was judging your chance of admission?
6. What would you be comfortable keeping private, sharing with an advisor, or sharing with peers?
7. Which cohort participation option felt most natural?
8. What would make you come back next week?
9. What would make you stop using it?
10. What should this be called?

Use a final one-minute retrieval prompt:

> Without looking back, write the three things from an experience that future-you will need when preparing an application.

A strong answer mentions some combination of accurate facts, role/action, impact/context, learning/growth, and meaning.

---

## 6. Quantitative pilot checks

- Startup explainer completion or intentional skip without help: target at least 90%.
- Core onboarding completion without help: target at least 80%.
- Median time to first meaningful artifact: target 10 minutes or less.
- Students who understand “facts plus reflection”: target at least 85%.
- Students who understand “not an admissions predictor”: target at least 90%.
- Students who can explain their branch recommendation: target at least 80%.
- Students who find a comfortable cohort entry mode: target at least 85%.
- Critical mobile or accessibility blockers: target zero.

Treat these as design targets for iteration, not student performance measures.

---

## 7. Presenter reveal after the test

After the debrief, explain the ecosystem in one minute:

> The tool connects five things that students often keep separate: courses, experiences, reflection, support, and learning habits. Over time, those records become evidence you can use in advising and application preparation. The game layer is there to help you return, notice growth, and support one another - never to rank you or tell you whether you will be admitted.

Then show the Journey Map, Experience Vault, Story Studio, Learning Lab, Support Constellation, and Cohort Commons as one connected system.

---

## 8. Go/no-go decision after the milestone

Proceed to a larger pilot only if:

- students independently understand the core value proposition;
- privacy and admissions boundaries are clear;
- the artifact quest works at phone width;
- branch recommendations feel explainable rather than judgmental;
- quiet participation is experienced as real participation;
- no critical accessibility blockers remain; and
- program owners agree on content, moderation, and student-data governance.

If the experience-to-reflection-to-application connection does not land, revise that loop before adding more features.
